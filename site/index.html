Fill and sign PDFs. That's it.

Paraphe fills in forms and adds text, ticks, today's date and your signature to any PDF, then saves a copy. It's free, there's no account and no ads, and your documents never leave your computer.

What it does

  • Fill in forms

    Click a field and type, like you always have. Amounts are tidied into your region's format.

  • Write anywhere

    For PDFs without fields: click where the text goes and type. Double-click it later to change it.

  • Tick or cross boxes

    Pick the tick or the cross, then click each box on the page.

  • Add today's date

    One click, written the way your region writes dates.

  • Sign

    Draw your signature, type it, or import a photo of it. Paraphe keeps it for next time.

  • Save a copy

    Ctrl+S saves "Form (signed).pdf" and leaves the original as it was.

Dates and amounts the way you write them

Paraphe follows your computer's region, and you can switch it from the dock. The saved file is named in your language too: "Form (signé).pdf" in French.

It's a PDF reader too, with the basics: search, zoom, page numbers, the table of contents, links and printing.

Your documents stay on your computer

The forms you sign are full of things you don't want on someone else's server: your address, your bank details, your signature. Paraphe never sends anything anywhere.

  • PDFs can carry scripts, a common way to attack PDF readers. Paraphe never runs them.
  • The page's security policy blocks every network request, so a document can't leave your computer, even by mistake.
  • No account and no analytics. The Windows app asks before it ever checks for updates. If you say yes, it asks GitHub once a day, and installs an update only if it carries Paraphe's signature.
  • The signatures you save stay on your computer.
  • The browser version works the same way: this site sends the page, and everything after that happens on your computer.
  • The source is on GitHub under the MIT licence, so anyone can check.

Get Paraphe

Windows

  1. Download Paraphe-win-x64.zip (__SIZE__).
  2. Extract it anywhere, your Documents folder for example.
  3. Run Paraphe.exe. There's nothing to install and no admin rights needed.

Windows warns you the first time because the app isn't signed with a paid certificate. Choose More info, then Run anyway.

To keep it, click Install on its start screen. To open all your PDFs with it, open Settings (the gear) and click Make Paraphe the default. You can uninstall it from there too.

In your browser

Open Paraphe in Edge, Chrome or Firefox and drop a PDF on it. Nothing to install.

Mac

A Mac version is in beta. For now it's built from the source: see the instructions.

What it doesn't do

  • Form scripts don't run, so a total the form would normally work out for you stays empty.
  • It adds a picture of your signature, not a certified digital signature. That's fine for a rental application, not for a contract that asks for a certificate.
  • Forms built only with XFA (some government forms) don't open properly.
  • Text you add can't be in Chinese, Japanese or Korean yet.

Why it exists

Last time I tried to add a date to a PDF, I got so sick of Acrobat Reader that I decided to write my own PDF editor. Filling and signing forms is all I ever needed it for.

Paraphe is French for the initials you write at the bottom of each page of a contract.
